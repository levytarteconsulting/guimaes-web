Compliance for SMEs: the obligations you cannot ignore

GGUIMAES · April 23, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • Compliance also applies to SMEs, not only to large companies.
  • Data protection (GDPR) is the most cross-cutting and monitored obligation.
  • Certain activities have specific anti-money-laundering obligations.
  • A criminal-compliance programme can mitigate or avoid the company's liability.
  • Preventing with simple measures costs far less than a penalty.

Contents

  1. What is compliance and who does it bind?
  2. The obligations you cannot ignore
  3. Criminal compliance: why it matters to an SME
  4. How to start without overwhelm

Compliance is no longer just for large companies. Any SME handles personal data, signs contracts, has employees and must meet a growing set of obligations whose breach is penalised. The good news is that most are solved with order and the right measures. This guide reviews the compliance obligations an SME cannot ignore and how to address them without turning it into a burden.

What is compliance and who does it bind?

Compliance is the set of policies and measures that ensure a company meets the regulations that apply to it and prevents legal risks. It is not a single formality but an approach: identify which obligations you have, implement measures to meet them and be able to prove it. And it is not exclusive to large companies: an SME handling data, hiring staff or operating in regulated sectors is also subject to it.

The obligations you cannot ignore

  • Data protection (GDPR and Spanish LOPDGDD): legal bases, information and security.
  • Anti-money-laundering in the obliged activities.
  • Labor obligations: risk prevention, equality and working-time records.
  • Tax and accounting obligations up to date and consistent.
  • Contracting and general terms compliant with consumer regulations.
  • Criminal compliance: preventing offences that could be committed on the company's behalf.

Criminal compliance: why it matters to an SME

Since companies can be criminally liable for certain offences committed for their benefit, having a prevention programme (a criminal-compliance model) can mitigate or even remove that liability. For an SME it does not mean building a department: it means having its risks identified, proportionate measures and the ability to prove it acts diligently. It is prevention, and far cheaper than a problem.

How to start without overwhelm

  1. Run a diagnosis of which obligations really apply to your activity.
  2. Prioritise the most cross-cutting and monitored: data protection and labor.
  3. Implement measures proportionate to your size, not those of a large firm.
  4. Document what you do: in compliance, being able to prove it is half the value.

Frequently asked questions

Does a small SME need compliance?+

Yes, though at a proportionate scale. Obligations such as data protection or labor rules apply to practically any company with clients and employees, and breaching them is penalised.

What happens if I do not comply with the GDPR?+

Data protection is among the most monitored and its penalties can be high. Beyond the financial risk, a badly handled incident damages your clients' trust. Putting it in order is relatively simple and highly advisable.

Is criminal compliance mandatory?+

It is not mandatory in the strict sense, but having a prevention programme can mitigate or remove the company's criminal liability. For many SMEs it is a cheap insurance policy against a serious risk.

Where do I start?+

With a diagnosis: which obligations really apply to your activity. From there you prioritise the most cross-cutting and implement measures proportionate to your size, without copying large-company models.
