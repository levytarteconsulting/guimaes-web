Inheritance tax in Madrid: the 99% relief and what it does not cover

GGUIMAES · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • In Madrid, spouses, children, grandchildren, parents and grandparents get 99% relief on the tax due; siblings, uncles, aunts, nephews and nieces get 50% since July 2025.
  • The relief is not automatic: it is applied in the self-assessment return, which is mandatory even if nothing is payable.
  • The deadline is six months from the death; an extension must be requested before the first five months are over.
  • For gifts, as a general rule, a public deed is required to apply the relief, and the deadline is 30 working days.
  • Municipal capital gains tax, notary, land registry and personal income tax are separate, and an inheritance is far better planned during one's lifetime.

"In Madrid, inheritances are tax-free." It is the sentence we correct most often at the firm, and the one that causes the most trouble when someone acts as if it were true.

The Community of Madrid has one of the most favourable inheritance tax regimes in Spain. That is true. But between "very favourable" and "nothing to do" there is a gap measured in missed deadlines, relief that is not applied and, sometimes, quite a lot of money.

This is what you need to know.

What the rules actually say

In Madrid a 99% relief on the tax due applies when the heirs are the deceased's spouse, descendants or ascendants. That is, Groups I and II of the tax: children, grandchildren, parents, grandparents and spouse. It has been in force since 2007.

For siblings, uncles, aunts, nephews and nieces, the relief is 50%, and it applies to deaths from July 1, 2025. Before that date it was 25%, and only for blood relatives.

Who inheritsRelief
Spouse, children, grandchildren, parents, grandparents99%
Siblings, uncles, aunts, nephews and nieces (and in-laws, since July 2025)50%
Other relatives and unrelated peopleNo regional relief

Look at the last row. An unregistered partner, a friend, a cousin: there is no relief there. And the state tax scale, combined with the multipliers applied to distant relatives, can produce very high figures.

The five mistakes we see again and again

1. Thinking 99% means 100%

It does not. 1% of the tax due is still payable. In small inheritances the amounts are token; in large estates, not so much. It is little, but it exists, and it has to be paid.

2. Thinking that if nothing is paid, nothing has to be filed

This is the most expensive mistake of all.

The self-assessment return is mandatory, whatever the amount payable. The relief does not apply by itself: it is applied in the return you file. If you do not file, there is no relief, and what follows is a tax audit procedure with a surcharge or penalty.

3. Letting the six months go by

The deadline to file for an inheritance is six months from the date of death.

You can request a further six-month extension, but there is a detail many people do not know: it must be requested before the first five months are over. If you remember in the sixth month, there is no extension left to request. And the extension also accrues late-payment interest.

Six months sounds like a lot. Between finding documents, obtaining certificates, valuing property and getting several heirs to agree, they fly by.

4. Making a gift without a public deed

When it comes to lifetime gifts, rather than inheritances, there is a formal requirement that sinks a lot of relief: as a general rule, the gift must be executed in a public deed.

A bank transfer from parent to child, on its own, does not meet that requirement. The money moves, the gift exists for tax purposes, but the relief cannot be applied.

And the deadline here is much shorter: 30 working days.

5. Confusing inheritance tax with the rest of the bill

The 99% relief covers Inheritance Tax. It does not cover everything else that comes with an inheritance:

  • Municipal capital gains tax (plusvalía municipal), if there is urban property, which is a local council tax and separate.
  • Notary and land registry fees for allocating and registering the assets.
  • The consequences for the heirs' personal income tax from then on, especially if they inherit let property or investment portfolios.
  • And, if the inherited assets are sold later, the capital gain, which is calculated on the value declared in the inheritance. Declaring a low value to save today can be costly when you sell tomorrow.

And if the estate is large, there is another piece

Madrid also grants 100% relief on Wealth Tax. But there is the Large Fortunes Tax, a state tax on net wealth above €3 million, at rates from 1.7% to 3.5%, from which any Wealth Tax paid is deducted.

The result: for estates above that threshold, the regional relief is no longer enough and the tax ends up being paid anyway. That is exactly the scenario where planning done in good time makes the difference.

What can be planned

And this is the part almost nobody considers, because it is assumed that in Madrid it is not needed.

It is. Things decided during one's lifetime that shape the bill afterwards:

  • How it is shared out, and whether it is better done by will, by staggered gifts or by a combination of both.
  • What form the estate takes: inheriting individual properties is not the same as inheriting shares in a company, especially when a family business is involved.
  • The family business, which has very significant specific reductions, with requirements that must be met before the death, not after.
  • The tax residence of the deceased and of the heirs, which determines which regional rules apply. Living in Madrid is not the same as being registered as a resident in Madrid.

This is the kind of work we do as part of tax planning: organising today what will determine tomorrow's bill.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to file for the inheritance even if nothing is payable?+

Yes. The self-assessment return is mandatory and the relief is applied within it. Not filing it is what creates the problem.

How long do I have?+

Six months from the death. You can request a further six-month extension, but it must be requested before the first five months are over, and it accrues late-payment interest.

I am the deceased's nephew or niece. Does the 99% apply to me?+

No. Siblings, uncles, aunts, nephews and nieces get 50%, for deaths from July 1, 2025.

My father wants to give me money during his lifetime. Is anything special needed?+

Yes: as a general rule, the gift must be executed in a public deed. Without it the relief is lost. The deadline to file is 30 working days.

I live outside Madrid but my father lived here. Which rules apply?+

For inheritances, as a general rule, those of the region where the deceased had their habitual residence in recent years. It is worth reviewing case by case, because it is one of the issues that causes the most disputes.

What if more than six months have passed and I filed nothing?+

File it on your own initiative, as soon as possible. It is always better than waiting for the tax authorities to demand it.

General information updated in November 2026, based on the rules of the Community of Madrid. Every inheritance is different: ask about your specific case.
