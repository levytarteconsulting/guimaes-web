Verifactu 2026: what changes and how to prepare your invoicing

Verifactu is the Spanish Tax Agency's verifiable-invoicing system that requires invoicing software to record every invoice in a complete, traceable and unalterable way. For most companies and freelancers it is not a minor formality: it means reviewing (and sometimes replacing) the software they invoice with every day. This guide explains exactly what changes, who it affects, the 2026 deadlines and how to prepare without last-minute stress.

What exactly is Verifactu?

Verifactu is the popular name for the system set out in Spain's Anti-Fraud Law and its regulation on invoicing software (Royal Decree 1007/2023). Its goal is to end the dual-use software that allowed sales to be hidden: once in force, every invoicing program must generate a record for each invoice, chained to the previous one through a fingerprint or hash, so that no invoice can be deleted or altered without leaving a trace.

There are two modes: the 'Verifactu' mode, in which your program sends records to the Tax Agency in real time, and the 'non-Verifactu' mode, in which records are kept locally but with stricter signing and tamper-proofing requirements. Most SMEs will choose the first for convenience.

Who does it affect and from when?

It affects almost all companies and freelancers who issue invoices through software, with some exceptions (for example, those already under the Immediate Supply of Information, SII). Entry into force is phased depending on the type of taxpayer and when your software provider adapts the product, so the exact date that applies to you should be confirmed with your advisor.

  • Companies subject to Corporate Income Tax: among the first to join.
  • Freelancers and professionals invoicing with software: later incorporation.
  • Those invoicing by hand or already in the SII: special cases to review.

What your invoicing software must comply with

  • Generate an invoicing record for each invoice, chained to the previous one.
  • Include a QR code on the invoice and the 'Verifactu' mention where applicable.
  • Guarantee the integrity, retention, traceability and tamper-proofing of records.
  • Be able to send records to the Tax Agency automatically (Verifactu mode).
  • Have a responsible declaration from the provider certifying compliance.
The key is not rushing in the last month: it is choosing the right software and adapting your process in advance.

How to prepare, step by step

  1. Confirm with your provider whether your current program will be Verifactu-compatible and on what date.
  2. Review your invoicing process: who issues invoices, from which devices and with which integrations.
  3. Plan the transition before your mandatory date, with room for testing.
  4. Train your team on the new workflow and document the procedure.
  5. Coordinate invoicing and accounting so the data reconciles from day one.

Common mistakes worth avoiding

The most common mistake is leaving it to the last minute and discovering the software is not updated or that the real workflow does not fit the tool. Another classic is assuming 'the accountant will handle it': Verifactu affects your daily operations, not just tax filing. A third is failing to check the provider's responsible declaration, which is your guarantee that the program complies.

Frequently asked questions

Is Verifactu mandatory for freelancers?+

Yes, for freelancers who issue invoices through software, except for cases such as those already reporting under the SII. The incorporation date depends on the taxpayer type and when your provider adapts the software.

Can I keep using Excel to invoice?+

A generic spreadsheet does not meet Verifactu's chaining and tamper-proofing requirements. You will need invoicing software that complies with the rule and has the provider's responsible declaration.

What is the difference between Verifactu and the SII?+

The SII is the near-real-time supply of VAT ledgers and mainly affects large companies and groups. Verifactu regulates how your software generates and protects each invoice. If you are already in the SII, Verifactu probably does not apply to you.

What happens if I do not adapt in time?+

Using or producing non-compliant software can lead to penalties. Also, continuing to invoice with a non-conforming program leaves you exposed in any review. That is why it is best to plan the transition in advance.
