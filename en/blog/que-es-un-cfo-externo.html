Outsourced CFO: what it is, what it does and how much it costs

GGUIMAES · December 2, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • An outsourced CFO is a part-time finance director: the same role as an in-house one, with the time commitment your company needs.
  • Outsourced CFO, external finance director, part-time CFO and fractional CFO are names for the same service.
  • They handle financial decisions (cash, margins, financing, growth) and do not replace the accounting: they build on it.
  • It is hired on a monthly fee, per project or by the day; one public reference for recurring engagements is €3,000 to €8,000 a month excluding VAT.
  • An in-house finance director in Spain costs between €85,000 and €170,000 gross a year, plus social security.

An outsourced CFO is a finance director who works for your company part-time, without being on the payroll. They provide the same function as an in-house finance director (cash control, profitability analysis, forecasting, relations with banks and investors), but devoting to your company the days it needs, not five a week.

The service goes by many names and it is worth clearing them up from the start, because they all mean the same thing: outsourced CFO, external finance director, outsourced financial management, part-time CFO and fractional CFO. In Spanish you will see it as CFO externo or director financiero externo.

This article explains what they do, how they differ from what you already have and how much they cost. If what you want to know is whether your company has reached the point of needing one, we cover that in this other article.

What exactly does an outsourced CFO do?

The difference between accounting and financial management is the same as between recording and deciding. Accounting tells you what has already happened. Financial management uses that information to decide what is going to happen.

These are its usual functions:

AreaWhat it does
Cash managementCash forecasting weeks and months ahead, working capital control, management of collections and payments
ProfitabilityReal margin by customer, product or business line; spotting what does not contribute
Budget and forecastsAnnual budget, scenarios, tracking of deviations
DashboardThe indicators that really matter in your business, up to date and easy to understand
FinancingPreparing for and negotiating with banks, debt structure, financing alternatives
Corporate transactionsInvestment rounds, buying and selling companies, partners joining or leaving
Pricing and costsCost structure, pricing policy, break-even point
Relations with the board or partnersA single, reliable reporting pack, so everyone discusses the same figures

Not every company needs all eight. A typical engagement starts with two or three and grows.

How is it different from what I already have?

This is the question that causes the most confusion, because the services partly overlap.

What it solvesWhat it does not solve
Gestoría (administrative agency)Filing taxes and payroll on timeDoes not analyse, forecast or advise on decisions
Tax and accounting advisory firmThe above, plus tax optimisation and complianceDoes not manage cash or the profitability of the business
In-house accountantUp-to-date bookkeeping and monthly closeDeciding or negotiating with banks is not their role
Outsourced CFOFinancial decisions: cash, margins, financing, growthDoes not replace the accounting: builds on it
In-house CFOAll of the above, full-timeHigh fixed cost; oversized for mid-sized companies

The relationship between the outsourced CFO and your advisory firm is not one of competition, but of layers: one needs the other to have the numbers right.

How much does an outsourced CFO cost?

It depends on the scope, the frequency and the profile. But there are three common engagement models:

Recurring monthly fee. The most common model. A number of days or a monthly time commitment and a fixed fee are agreed. It provides continuity and works best when the goal is to build a stable finance function.

Per project. For a specific goal with a beginning and an end: preparing a funding round, negotiating with banks, implementing a dashboard, a due diligence. It is quoted at a fixed price.

By the day or by the hour. For companies that only need occasional support, or as a way to start before committing to a fee.

The figures, in context

Be wary of anyone who quotes a price without knowing anything about your company. But some references can be given.

A specialised Spanish firm openly publishes a range of €3,000 to €8,000 a month excluding VAT for recurring engagements, expressly warning that it describes its own offering and not a market average (Iter Advisors). For smaller engagements, the range drops considerably.

The relevant comparison is with the alternative. According to the salary survey by VYNE HR, based on more than 40 real recruitment processes, an in-house finance director in Spain sits in 2026 in a band of €85,000 to €170,000 gross a year, with a median of €120,000. On top of that come the employer's social security contributions, which raise the real cost significantly.

In other words: the question is not whether an outsourced CFO is expensive, but at what level of commitment it stops paying off compared with hiring one. For most SMEs, that point is quite a bit higher than most people assume.

When does it make sense to hire one?

In short: when the complexity of the business has outgrown what a spreadsheet and the founder's instinct can handle. The typical signs are that you are selling more but cash is not keeping up, that you do not know which customers make you money, that a bank is asking for numbers you do not have, or that a transaction is coming up.

We go into it with a seven-sign test in When your SME needs an outsourced CFO (and when it doesn't), including the cases where it is not yet worth it.

How do you work with an outsourced CFO?

A well-designed engagement usually follows this order:

  1. Diagnosis. Understanding the business, reviewing the information available and identifying what is missing. Usually the first few weeks.
  2. Laying the foundations. A cash forecast and a basic dashboard. This is what gives immediate control.
  3. Analysing profitability. Margins by customer, product or line. This is where the surprises usually appear.
  4. Planning. Budget, scenarios and the decisions that follow from them.
  5. Ongoing support. Regular reviews, support with decisions and negotiations as they arise.

Results arrive at different speeds: cash control shows within weeks; better profitability and access to financing, within months.

Frequently asked questions

What is an outsourced CFO?+

A finance director who serves a company part-time, without being on its payroll. They take on the financial management functions (cash, profitability, forecasting, financing) with the time commitment the company needs.

Is it the same as a part-time CFO or a fractional CFO?+

Yes. Outsourced CFO, external finance director, outsourced financial management, part-time CFO and fractional CFO are different names for the same service.

How much does an outsourced CFO cost in Spain?+

It depends on the scope and time commitment. As a public reference, one specialised firm publishes a range of €3,000 to €8,000 a month excluding VAT for recurring engagements, and makes clear that this is its own offering and not a market average. For smaller scopes, the cost is lower. The useful comparison is with an in-house finance director, who in Spain in 2026 costs between €85,000 and €170,000 gross a year plus social security.

Does it replace my advisory firm or my accountant?+

No. It works on the information they produce. The advisory firm handles tax and accounting compliance; the outsourced CFO handles financial decisions.

From what turnover does it make sense?+

It does not depend on turnover, but on complexity and what is at stake. An €800,000 company with tight margins, stock and several business lines needs one more than a €3 million company with two stable customers and plenty of cash.

Can I hire one just for a specific transaction?+

Yes. It is one of the most common uses: preparing a funding round, a sale, a new partner coming in or a bank negotiation.

How long until the effect shows?+

Cash control, a few weeks. Profitability analysis and its consequences, a few months. Better financing terms, when it is time to renew.

What does it need from my company to get started?+

Reasonably up-to-date accounts and access to sales, cost and bank information. If the accounts are behind, the first step is to bring them up to date: without reliable data there can be no financial management.
