Shareholder agreement: the document that prevents the worst disputes

GGUIMAES · May 14, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • The shareholder agreement governs the relationship between partners beyond what the bylaws say.
  • It is confidential and flexible; the bylaws are public and more rigid.
  • It must anticipate a partner's exit, incoming money and deadlock in decisions.
  • Signing it early, when there is goodwill, is what makes it useful.
  • A good agreement prevents disputes that, without it, end up in court.

The shareholder agreement is the document that decides what happens when things get difficult… signed while they are still going well. It governs how decisions are made, what happens if a partner leaves, how money comes in or out and how disagreements are resolved. It is cheap and simple to sign at the start, and hugely expensive to resolve once the conflict has erupted. Here we explain what it must include and why you should not leave it for later.

What is it and what is it for?

A shareholder agreement is a private contract between a company's partners that governs their relationship: how the company is run, what majorities are needed to decide, how profits are shared, what happens if someone wants to sell their stake or if a partner stops contributing. It complements the bylaws and lets you agree things they do not cover, with far more flexibility and confidentially.

Shareholder agreement vs bylaws

The bylaws are the company's public framework, filed in the Companies Registry and relatively rigid. The shareholder agreement is private, not published and can adapt to the partners' specific reality. Many sensitive issues —how an exit is valued, what happens in a partner's divorce, lock-in clauses— fit better in the agreement than in the bylaws.

What a good agreement must include

  • Governance and decision-making: reinforced majorities for key matters.
  • Regime for transferring shares: pre-emption rights.
  • Drag-along and tag-along clauses in the event of a sale.
  • What happens if a partner leaves, dies or stops meeting their commitment.
  • Mechanisms to resolve deadlock and disputes without going to court.
  • Commitments on dedication, non-compete and confidentiality.

When to sign it?

The short answer: as soon as possible. The best moment is when incorporating the company or when a new partner comes in, while the relationship is good and no one is defensive. Trying to agree the rules once there is already conflict is almost impossible: everyone defends their own interest and what should prevent the problem becomes the problem. Signing early is cheap insurance against an expensive fire.

Frequently asked questions

Is a shareholder agreement mandatory?+

It is not legally required, but it is highly advisable as soon as there is more than one partner. Its absence is one of the main causes of serious corporate disputes.

What happens if a partner wants to leave and there is no agreement?+

Without an agreement, the bylaws and the law apply, which often do not resolve well how that exit is valued and settled. The result is usually a tense negotiation or outright litigation.

Is the shareholder agreement public?+

No. Unlike the bylaws, the agreement is a private document between partners and is not filed in the Companies Registry, which allows sensitive matters to be regulated confidentially.

Can it be amended later?+

Yes, with the agreement of the signing partners. In fact it is wise to review it when a new partner joins, the business changes or a round or sale is being prepared.
