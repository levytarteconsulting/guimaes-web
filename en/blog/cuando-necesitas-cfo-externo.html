When your SME needs an outsourced CFO (and when not)

GGUIMAES · June 12, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • What sets the moment is not turnover, but complexity and what is at stake.
  • Typical sign: you are selling more, but you don't know in advance whether you will have cash next month.
  • A transaction on the horizon —a round, a loan, an acquisition or a new partner— is often the trigger.
  • If you recognise three or more signs, it is worth talking about.
  • If your business is small and stable, you may not need one yet: we will tell you.

An outsourced CFO is a finance director who works for your company part-time, without the cost of an executive on the payroll. If you are looking for exactly what it is, what it does and how much it costs, we explain it here. The question is not whether it is useful, but when your company has reached the point where it needs one. This article focuses on that: the signs that the time has come and the cases where it is not yet. What exactly they do, how they differ from your bookkeeper and how to hire one is explained on the page for our outsourced CFO service.

Seven signs you already need one

1. You are selling more, but cash isn't keeping up

Growth consumes cash: more stock, more credit to customers, hiring before you get paid. If every month you look at the bank with some unease even though the profit and loss account says you are making money, you are missing someone to forecast cash weeks ahead. We cover this in depth in cash flow and liquidity.

2. Big decisions are made by gut feeling

Hiring, raising prices, opening a new line or buying machinery. If those decisions are made without a numerical scenario behind them, the risk is not getting it wrong once, but not knowing why it went well or badly.

3. You don't know which customers or products make you money

Accounting gives you the overall result, but not always the margin by customer, product or business line. It is common to discover that part of your revenue barely leaves any margin, or even loses money.

4. A bank or investor asks for numbers you don't have

Forecasts, a budget, a cash plan, ratios. When someone outside asks for that information and nobody in the company can prepare it convincingly, you negotiate at a disadvantage.

5. A transaction is coming up

A funding round, the acquisition of another company, a partner joining or leaving, or a restructuring. These are the moments when most value is won or lost through lack of financial preparation.

6. The founder is putting out financial fires

If whoever runs the company spends a good part of the week juggling payments, chasing collections or preparing reports, they are doing work someone else could do better and neglecting their own.

7. Partners argue with different figures

When each partner has their own version of how the business is doing, the arguments are not about strategy but about which number is right. A single, reliable report changes the conversation.

A quick test

Go through the seven signs. If you recognise one or two, it is probably enough to strengthen your accounting and check the cash forecast from time to time. If you recognise three or more, or if there is a transaction on the horizon in the coming months, it is time to consider financial management, even part-time.

When it is NOT worth it yet

Let's be honest: if you are a sole trader or a micro-business with stable income, few customers and no plans for growth or funding, an outsourced CFO is probably premature. In that case, good accounting and a close tax adviser are enough. We'd rather tell you that than sell you something that doesn't add value yet.

Nor does it make sense if all you want is someone to file your taxes: that is the job of a good bookkeeping or advisory firm. We explain the difference between the two approaches in traditional bookkeeper vs strategic advisor.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an outsourced CFO cost?+

Much less than an in-house finance director, because you only pay for the time you need. The cost depends on the scope: controlling cash a few hours a month is not the same as supporting an investment round.

From what size does it make sense?+

There is no magic figure; it depends more on complexity and plans than on turnover. If you are growing, making significant decisions or looking for funding, it usually makes sense even if you are small.

Can I hire one just for a specific transaction?+

Yes. Some companies bring in an outsourced CFO to prepare a round, negotiate with a bank or support an acquisition, and then decide whether to keep regular follow-up.

How soon are results noticeable?+

Cash control and clarity in the numbers are usually felt within the first few weeks. Profitability and funding decisions pay off over the first months.
