Hiring your first employee: a guide to get it right

GGUIMAES · June 5, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • The real cost of an employee includes salary, employer social security and extras.
  • The contract type determines incentives, severance and flexibility.
  • Social security registration must be done before the employee starts working.
  • There are incentives depending on profile and contract type worth taking advantage of.
  • Your sector's collective agreement sets minimum salary, working hours and categories.

Contents

  1. The real cost of hiring (beyond the salary)
  2. Which type of contract to choose
  3. Steps to register your first employee

Hiring your first employee is a big step, and also the moment when most mistakes are made through inexperience. The real cost of a worker goes well beyond the salary, the contract type conditions incentives and flexibility, and a badly done registration can be costly. In this guide we review everything you need to know to take the step with confidence.

The real cost of hiring (beyond the salary)

The most common mistake is thinking only of the gross salary. To that amount you must add the employer's social security contribution, which significantly increases the cost, plus extra payments, holidays and other items under the collective agreement. Before hiring, it is worth calculating the total annual cost to know whether the role is sustainable with your numbers.

Which type of contract to choose

  • Permanent: the general rule after the labor reform and gives access to incentives.
  • Temporary: only for specific, justified reasons; misuse carries penalties.
  • Training contracts: for young people or to combine study and practice, with incentives.
  • Full-time or part-time, depending on the real need of the role.

Steps to register your first employee

  1. If you are not already, register your company as an employer with social security.
  2. Check the applicable collective agreement: salary, hours and category.
  3. Choose the contract type and review the incentives you can apply for.
  4. Register the worker with social security before their first day.
  5. Formalise and report the contract, and comply with risk prevention and data protection.

Common mistakes when hiring for the first time

  • Letting the employee start before being registered.
  • Using a temporary contract with no real justifying reason.
  • Ignoring the collective agreement and paying below what is due.
  • Not using incentives one was entitled to.
  • Forgetting risk-prevention and data-protection obligations.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an employee really cost?+

Beyond the gross salary, the company pays the social security contribution, which raises the cost significantly. As a rough reference, the total cost is usually clearly above the gross salary. It should be calculated case by case.

Can I use a temporary contract as a trial?+

For a 'trial' there is the probation period within a permanent contract, not the temporary contract. Temporary contracts are only valid for specific reasons; using them as a disguised trial can lead to penalties.

What incentives are available?+

They depend on the worker's profile and contract type: there are incentives for permanent contracts, young people, certain groups and training contracts. They change frequently, so check the current ones before hiring.

Do I need a labor advisor for just one person?+

It is advisable: registration, the collective agreement, payroll and periodic obligations have their complexity, and a mistake with the first employee is usually the most expensive. An advisor saves you time and risk.
