Mandatory e-invoicing: what it is, when it arrives and how to prepare before 2027

GGUIMAES · May 26, 2026 · 7 min read

Update: the Spanish Ministry of Finance has announced a new timeline. Read the updated article.

In short

  • B2B e-invoicing will be mandatory between companies and freelancers in Spain.
  • It changes the format and delivery channel, not just the internal record like Verifactu.
  • Large invoicers enter first; the rest with more time (around 2027).
  • You must be able to issue, receive and report invoice statuses in a structured format.
  • Adapting your software and client data early avoids payment blockages.

Mandatory e-invoicing between companies and freelancers (B2B) is one of the big changes arriving with Spain's Crea y Crece Law. Unlike Verifactu, which regulates how your software records invoices, mandatory e-invoicing changes the format in which you send and receive them: the loose PDF by email is over. This guide clarifies what it is, when it arrives, how it differs from Verifactu and how to be ready before 2027.

What is mandatory e-invoicing?

It is the obligation to issue and receive invoices between companies and freelancers in a structured electronic format (not a simple PDF), through platforms that allow their exchange and the reporting of payment statuses. It stems from the Crea y Crece Law, aimed at reducing late payment and digitising commercial relations. It differs from the invoice you already issue to the public administration (Face), because it now extends to transactions between private companies.

E-invoicing vs Verifactu: they are not the same

This is the most common confusion. Verifactu regulates how your program generates, chains and protects each invoice record. Mandatory e-invoicing regulates the format and channel in which that invoice travels to your client and returns with its payment status. They are two distinct obligations that coexist: your system will need to comply with both.

When does it come into force?

The final timetable depends on the regulatory development, but the criterion is phased by annual turnover: larger companies will be required first, and SMEs and freelancers will have a longer period, around 2027. It is wise not to wait until the last moment, because you will need both you and your clients to be operational.

How to prepare before 2027

  1. Choose an invoicing solution that issues and receives in structured format and reports statuses.
  2. Clean up your client and supplier database: identifiers, emails and payment terms.
  3. Integrate invoicing with accounting to avoid duplication and mismatches.
  4. Talk to your key clients and suppliers to coordinate the transition.

Beyond the obligation: what you gain

Although it arrives as a requirement, e-invoicing done well reduces errors, speeds up collections and gives you real-time visibility of which invoices are accepted, paid or pending. For a growing company, that control over collection is gold: it improves cash flow without selling more.

Frequently asked questions

Does a PDF by email count as an e-invoice?+

Not in the sense of the new obligation. A PDF is an image of the document; mandatory e-invoicing requires a structured format that systems can process automatically and whose payment status is reported.

Does it affect invoices to private consumers?+

The obligation focuses on transactions between companies and freelancers (B2B). Sales to final consumers follow other rules, though each case should be reviewed with your advisor.

Do I necessarily have to change software?+

Not always: many providers are adapting their products. The important thing is to confirm your solution will issue and receive in structured format and report statuses within the deadline that applies to you.

How does it relate to late payment?+

The law seeks to reduce late payment by making issue, acceptance and payment dates traceable. Having those statuses recorded makes it easier to claim and, where applicable, apply consequences for delay.
