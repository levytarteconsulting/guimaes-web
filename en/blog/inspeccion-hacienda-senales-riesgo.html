Tax inspections: risk signals and how to stay ahead

GGUIMAES · June 2, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • The tax office cross-checks data from clients, banks, tax forms and third parties to spot inconsistencies.
  • Abnormal margins, recurring losses or heavy cash use raise the risk.
  • Consistency across tax forms (VAT, income, corporate) is the first thing reviewed.
  • Documenting and keeping supporting evidence is your best defence in a review.
  • Getting ahead with an internal review costs far less than fixing things afterwards.

A tax inspection does not always come by chance: the Tax Agency cross-checks more and more data and prioritises cases with risk signals. The good news is that most of those signals are avoidable with order and consistency. In this article we explain what usually triggers a review, which indicators the administration watches and how to get ahead so that, if it comes, you face it calmly.

How the tax office chooses who to review

The Tax Agency works with control plans and systems that analyse large volumes of information. It receives data from your clients and suppliers, banks, payment platforms and other administrations. When something does not add up —your declared sales do not match what your clients report, or your lifestyle does not match your income— an alert is raised. It is not persecution: it is data matching.

The most common risk signals in SMEs

  • Margins or ratios far from your sector average.
  • Losses declared year after year in an activity that keeps operating.
  • Heavy use of cash or mismatches between what is collected and declared.
  • Inconsistencies between VAT, income tax and corporate tax.
  • High or recurring VAT refunds without clear support.
  • Related-party transactions without transfer-pricing documentation.

How to get ahead before the letter arrives

  1. Review the consistency between all your tax forms and your accounts each quarter.
  2. Organise and keep supporting documents: invoices, contracts and proof of expenses.
  3. Document the criteria applied to mixed expenses and related-party transactions.
  4. Run an annual internal review with your advisor to spot weak points.

What to do if you receive a notice

First: do not ignore it and do not respond in the heat of the moment. Every communication has deadlines and a specific scope (one tax, one year, one item). You should understand exactly what is being asked, gather the documentation with your advisor and respond in an orderly manner and on time. A well-prepared response usually resolves most reviews without going further.

Frequently asked questions

How many years back can the tax office review?+

As a general rule, the non-time-barred years are the last four, although there are cases that extend that period. That is why you should keep documentation for at least that time.

Do losses trigger an inspection?+

Occasional losses are normal. What raises alerts is declaring recurring losses in an activity that keeps operating and sustaining a lifestyle, because it suggests an inconsistency you should be able to explain.

Is having an advisor mandatory in an inspection?+

It is not mandatory, but it is highly advisable. An advisor understands the real scope of what is being asked, prepares the documentation and avoids responses that complicate the process. It makes a difference to the outcome.

Can I completely avoid the risk of inspection?+

Avoiding it 100% is not in your hands, but you can greatly reduce the likelihood and, above all, arrive prepared. With consistent accounts and documentation in order, a review stops being a problem and becomes a formality.
