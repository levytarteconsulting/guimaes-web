VERI*FACTU is postponed to October 2028: what really changes and what doesn't

GGUIMAES · October 16, 2026 · 5 min read

In short

  • On October 5, the Ministry of Finance announced that VERI*FACTU is postponed to October 2028, for companies and all other taxpayers alike.
  • For now it is an announcement: until the amendment to Royal Decree 1007/2023 is published in the Official State Gazette, the enforceable dates are still those of 2027.
  • The when changes, not the what: invoicing records will still have to be complete, traceable and unalterable.
  • The aim is to make it coincide with mandatory B2B e-invoicing, so there is a single transition.
  • If you were already adapting, don't undo anything. If you had not started, use the extra time to do it properly.

The Ministry of Finance has announced that VERI*FACTU is delayed until October 2028. But it pays to read the small print: as of today, the regulation in force still says 2027.

On October 5, 2026, the Ministry of Finance published an information note announcing the postponement of the outstanding obligations under the VERI*FACTU regulation. The stated aim is to achieve the greatest possible convergence with mandatory B2B e-invoicing, both legally and technically.

It is good news for many companies that were cutting it fine. But there are three caveats worth understanding before you put the subject away in a drawer.

The timeline: what applied and what has been announced

Who it applies toDate in the current regulationAnnounced date
Companies (Corporate Tax payers)January 1, 2027October 2028
All other taxpayers (sole traders, joint ownerships…)July 1, 2027October 2028

Both groups converge on the same date. The note does not specify the exact day in October 2028.

Caveat 1: it is not yet law in force

This is the part we are being asked about most these days, and the one that matters most.

An information note from the Ministry does not amend a regulation. Until the amendment to Royal Decree 1007/2023 is published in the Official State Gazette (BOE), the legally enforceable dates are still those of 2027.

In practice, nobody expects the Ministry to announce a postponement and then not put it through. But a company that makes investment decisions, signs contracts with its software provider or plans internally on the basis of an announcement that has not yet been formalised is taking on a risk it should be aware of.

The prudent approach: plan with the new date as a reasonable horizon, without dismantling what was already under way, and confirm the final timeline once the regulation is published.

Caveat 2: postponing is not cancelling

The postponement changes the when, not the what.

According to the note itself, the substantive requirements remain in substantially equivalent terms: invoicing records will still have to be complete, traceable and unalterable. In other words, the underlying obligation (that your invoicing software must not allow an invoice to be changed or deleted without leaving a trace) does not go away.

Anyone who assumed the whole thing would be dropped is mistaken. The only thing that has changed is the time you have to get there.

Caveat 3: the reason for the delay shows where all this is heading

The postponement is not a U-turn: it is an attempt to make two obligations that were on separate tracks arrive together.

On one side, VERI*FACTU, which regulates how your invoicing software must behave. On the other, mandatory B2B e-invoicing, which regulates the format in which invoices are issued and sent, and whose timeline depends on size: companies with turnover above €8 million come in before the rest.

Forcing companies through two technology changes in a row, a year and a half apart, did not make much sense. Aligning them allows for a single transition.

And there is a third horizon behind all this: the European harmonisation of VAT in the digital age, which will set the final destination for both rules. What we are seeing is not a system being postponed, but a system being built in phases.

So, what should I do now?

If you had already adapted or were in the process, don't undo anything. The Tax Agency describes the period before the obligation as a testing phase in which a compliant system can be used voluntarily. Getting there early carries no penalty: it lets you find problems without the pressure of a deadline.

If you had not started, treat it as what it is: time, not a pardon. Two years give you room to do properly something that goes wrong when rushed. And remember that adapting is not just a matter of changing software: it means reviewing how invoices are issued in your company, who issues them and with what data.

Talk to your software provider. Most invoicing programs have already built in the requirements or are in the process of doing so. Ask about their timeline, not their intentions.

And use the opportunity to ask the bigger question. If you are going to touch your invoicing system anyway, it is worth asking whether the one you have is the one you need, or whether this is the moment to move to truly digital management. We explain it in this other article.
