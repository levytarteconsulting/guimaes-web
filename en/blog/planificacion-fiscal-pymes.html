Tax planning for SMEs: how to pay less tax, legally

GGUIMAES · May 7, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

  • Planning (legal) differs from evading (illegal): the key is the real economic reason.
  • The right legal form can significantly change your tax burden.
  • Deductions, depreciation and incentives are usually underused.
  • The timing of decisions is a lever as powerful as it is forgotten.
  • Planning is done during the year, not when the return is due.

Planning taxes is not evading: it is organising your activity to pay what is due, not a euro more. The law offers legitimate options —legal form, deductions, timing of decisions, partner remuneration— and choosing well among them is perfectly legal. The gap between a company that plans and one that does not can be large by year-end. In this guide we explain the real levers, always within the law.

Tax planning: what it is and why it is legal

Tax planning means using the options the law itself provides to organise your activity in the most efficient way. It is legal as long as it responds to a real economic reason and not to an artificial arrangement whose sole purpose is to pay less. That is the line separating planning (legitimate) from abusive avoidance or evasion (illegal). A good advisor moves comfortably within that line and does not cross it.

The main saving levers

  • Legal form: sole trader, company or group structure depending on your profit level.
  • Partner and director remuneration: balance between salary, dividends and other routes (see company structure and your income tax return).
  • Deductions and incentives: R&D&i, investments, training, hiring.
  • Depreciation and free depreciation where the rule allows.
  • Offsetting losses from previous years.
  • Timing: in which year you allocate income, expenses and investments.

The legal form: the highest-impact decision

Above a certain profit level, being taxed as a company rather than as a sole trader can make a significant difference, because the Corporate Income Tax rate and the personal income tax rate work differently. There is no universal answer: it depends on how much you earn, how much you reinvest and how you pay yourself. It is one of the first things to review with an advisor, because the potential saving recurs year after year.

Timing matters: the forgotten lever

Many tax decisions depend on the when: bringing forward or delaying an investment, allocating an expense to one year or the next, distributing dividends this year or next. These decisions can only be made in time, not on the return. That is why tax planning is continuous work throughout the year, with a reinforced review before the close.

Frequently asked questions

Is tax planning legal?+

Yes, provided it is based on options the law provides and responds to a real economic reason. What is illegal is evasion (concealing) and abusive avoidance (artificial arrangements with no substance). Planning well is precisely staying within the law.

From what point is creating a company worth it?+

It depends on profit, how much you reinvest and how you pay yourself; there is no single figure valid for everyone. It is a calculation worth doing with an advisor, because the saving repeats every year.

When should I plan?+

Throughout the year, with a special review before December 31. Waiting for the return is too late: by then most decisions can no longer be taken.

Does tax planning increase the risk of inspection?+

Planning well, with an economic reason and documentation, does not increase the risk; on the contrary, it tidies your situation. What does increase it are aggressive arrangements with no substance, which a good advisor avoids.
