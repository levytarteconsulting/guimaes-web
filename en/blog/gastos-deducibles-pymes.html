Deductible expenses many SMEs overlook

GGUIMAES · June 24, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • To deduct an expense it must be linked to the activity, documented and booked.
  • Training, software, premises utilities or part of the vehicle are often forgotten.
  • A full invoice in the company's name is your best defence; a receipt is not always enough.
  • Mixed expenses (personal and professional) require judgement and proof of the business portion.
  • Good, up-to-date bookkeeping is what turns an expense into real savings.

Contents

  2. The most forgotten deductible expenses
  3. The tricky ground: mixed expenses
  4. Mistakes that cost you the deduction

Many SMEs pay more tax than necessary simply because they do not deduct expenses they are entitled to. We are not talking about tricks or grey areas, but perfectly legitimate expenses left out through ignorance, lack of a receipt or poor bookkeeping. In this article we review the most overlooked deductible expenses and the requirements so the tax office does not challenge them.

When an expense is really deductible

Before the list, the golden rule. An expense is deductible when it meets four conditions: it is correlated with income (necessary for the activity), justified with a full invoice, recorded in the accounts and allocated to the correct financial year. If one of them fails, the tax office can reject it, however real the expense is.

The most forgotten deductible expenses

  • Training and professional subscriptions: courses, professional bodies, software and SaaS tools.
  • Utilities of the premises used for the activity: electricity, water, internet, cleaning and maintenance.
  • Proportional part of the home if you work from home (with reasoned criteria and proof).
  • Vehicle and fuel costs in the portion used for the economic activity.
  • Insurance, bank fees, advisory services and incorporation or notary costs.
  • Depreciation of equipment, furniture and assets that lose value with use.

The tricky ground: mixed expenses

Meals, phone, vehicle or home use are expenses that mix the personal and the professional. Here you cannot simply deduct 100%: you must apply a reasonable business-use criterion and be able to prove it. For example, with business meals the amount, context and counterpart matter; with the vehicle, its real use for the activity. An advisor helps you set a defensible criterion and document it.

Mistakes that cost you the deduction

  • Keeping the receipt instead of asking for a full invoice in the company's name.
  • Paying large expenses in cash with no bank trail.
  • Including clearly personal expenses that will not survive a review (the answer is to properly separate personal and business finances).
  • Not booking on time and losing invoices along the way.

Frequently asked questions

Can I deduct business meals?+

Yes, if they are related to the activity, justified with an invoice and reasonable. There are limits, and it is wise to document the reason and who attended so you can defend it.

Is a receipt enough or do I need an invoice?+

To deduct safely you should have a full invoice with your tax details. A simplified receipt offers fewer guarantees and for many expenses the tax office can reject the deduction.

Can I deduct costs of working from home?+

You can deduct the proportional part of utilities used for the activity, applying the percentage of the home used for work. It is a monitored expense, so the criterion must be applied correctly and evidence kept.

What about the VAT on those expenses?+

VAT deduction follows its own, sometimes stricter, rules than the expense in income or corporate tax. Some expenses allow you to deduct the cost but not all the VAT. Each case should be reviewed.
