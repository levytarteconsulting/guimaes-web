Form 232: who has to file it and what happens if you don't

GGUIMAES · November 3, 2026 · 8 min read

In short

  • Renting premises to your company, lending it money or invoicing it for services are related-party transactions: they must be valued at market price.
  • Form 232 must be filed if you exceed €250,000 with the same person or entity, €100,000 in specific transactions, or any amount with tax havens.
  • If your company's financial year ends on December 31, the filing window runs from November 1 to 30.
  • Reporting and documenting are separate obligations: the duty to document applies even if you don't reach any threshold.
  • Failing to document is penalised even if you haven't underpaid: €1,000 per item of data and €10,000 per data set.

If you rent premises to your own company, lend it money or invoice it for services, you are carrying out related-party transactions. And you probably have obligations you didn't know about.

It is one of the most common situations in Spanish SMEs and one of the least understood. The director who owns the premises the company operates from and rents them to it. The partner who lends money to their company to get through a tight cash spell. Two companies with the same owner invoicing each other for services. The family group with one holding company for assets and another for operations.

All of these are related-party transactions. And the law requires two things: valuing them at market price and, above certain amounts, reporting them on Form 232.

For companies whose financial year ended on December 31, the deadline runs from November 1 to 30.

What a related-party transaction is

Any economic transaction between parties linked by control or family ties. The most common in an SME:

  • Between a company and its shareholders holding 25% or more.
  • Between a company and its directors, except for the pay received for acting as director.
  • Between companies in the same group, including those that share an owner without formally being a group.
  • Between a company and the spouses or relatives, up to the third degree, of its shareholders or directors.

The rationale is simple: when both sides of a transaction are really the same person, the price can be set at will, and with it, where the profit is taxed. That is why the Tax Agency requires them to be valued as if they had been agreed between independent parties.

Who has to file Form 232

Not every related-party transaction is reported. They are reported when any of these thresholds is exceeded:

CaseThreshold
Transactions with the same related person or entityOver €250,000 in the financial year, at market value
Specific transactions of the same type (real estate, intangibles, transfers of businesses or unlisted shares, and with people taxed under the flat-rate regime who hold at least 25%)Over €100,000
Transactions of the same type and valuation methodIf together they exceed 50% of turnover, whatever the amount
Transactions with tax havensAny amount

Form 232 is also used to report the reduction on income from certain intangible assets, known as the patent box.

Excluded, among others, are transactions within a tax consolidation group and most of those carried out by economic interest groupings (AIE) and joint ventures (UTE) with their members.

The costliest mistake: confusing reporting with documenting

This is the point almost everyone misses, and it is the one that really costs money.

They are two separate, independent obligations.

The reporting obligation is Form 232, and it only arises if you exceed the thresholds above.

The documentation obligation means having a file ready that shows those transactions were valued at market price, and it applies even if you don't reach any threshold and don't file Form 232. Entities with turnover below €10 million do have a simplified documentation regime, and there are even cases of exemption, but that doesn't mean you can get away with justifying nothing.

In other words: you may not be required to file the form and still be required to prove, if asked, that the rent you charge your company is the market rate and not a figure chosen for tax convenience.

Penalties don't work the way you expect

This is the part worth understanding well, because the logic is different from other tax penalties.

For not filing Form 232, the regime for information returns applies: €20 for each omitted or inaccurate item or set of data, with a minimum of €300 and a maximum of €20,000. And there is a nuance worth knowing: if you file late on your own initiative, before the Tax Agency asks for it, the penalty and its limits are halved.

For not documenting the transactions, the regime is much harsher: €1,000 for each omitted item and €10,000 per data set, where the Administration makes no valuation adjustment. If the Tax Agency does adjust the valuation, the penalty is 15% of the adjustment.

Note the key point: in the first case the fine doesn't depend on whether you underpaid. You can have all your taxes correctly settled, not owe the Tax Agency a single euro, and still be penalised just for not having the file ready. It is not the financial loss that is penalised: it is the formal breach.

There is a cap for that case, calculated on the amount of the transactions and on turnover, as well as reductions for agreement and prompt payment. But the starting point is what it is.

Why the Tax Agency looks at this more and more

Related-party transactions have been among the Tax Agency's audit priorities for years, and there is a technical reason: Form 232 is an information return that cross-checks itself.

If your company reports that it paid you €30,000 in rent and that income doesn't appear in your personal tax return, the cross-check is automatic. The same happens if two related companies report the same transaction for different amounts. It doesn't take a tax inspection to spot it.

And there is a second reason: it is the area where most small companies fall short without any bad faith, simply because nobody told them that what they are doing has a name and obligations.

What to review this month

If your company's financial year ended on December 31, you have until November 30. This is what to check:

  • List every related-party transaction in the year. Rents, loans, services, shareholders' salaries, asset sales, any movement with shareholders, directors, their relatives or other group companies.
  • Add them up by person or entity. The €250,000 threshold is measured per counterparty, not in total.
  • Review the specific transactions separately, because their threshold is lower: €100,000.
  • Check how each one was valued and whether anything supports it: an appraisal, market comparables, a report.
  • Check consistency between what the company reports and what the people on the other side report.

An opportunity, not just an obligation

A point that is rarely made: properly reviewing your related-party transactions often uncovers opportunities, not just risks.

Many companies have below-market rent on their premises, poorly structured director pay or intra-group loans with no agreed interest. Correcting that isn't just compliance: in many cases it improves the tax position of the whole, because it lets you place expenses and income where they make most sense within the rules.

It is a review that fits especially well if you have a holding structure or several companies, where transactions between group entities are constant and tend to be the ones most worth putting in order.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to file Form 232 if I am a sole trader?+

No. Form 232 is filed by Corporate Tax payers, by Non-Resident Income Tax payers operating through a permanent establishment and by certain pass-through entities. A sole trader doesn't file it, although they can be the related party in transactions reported by a company.

What if I don't reach any threshold?+

You don't file the form, but the obligation to value at market price and to be able to justify it still applies.

I rent premises to my own company. Is that a related-party transaction?+

Yes, if you hold at least 25% or are a director. It must be valued at market price, and it will be reported on Form 232 if the thresholds are exceeded.

What if I realise late that I should have filed it?+

Filing late on your own initiative is always better than waiting for a formal request: the penalty and its minimum and maximum limits are halved. The sooner, the better.

Is director pay a related-party transaction?+

Pay for acting as a director is expressly excluded. But if you also provide services to the company in another capacity, that part can be.
