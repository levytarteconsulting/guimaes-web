Tax benefit

The "Ley Beckham": your best tax ally when settling in Spain

If you move to Spain from LATAM for professional reasons, the special inbound-expatriate regime — known as the "Ley Beckham" — can significantly reduce your tax burden during your first years in the country.

01

What it is

An optional tax regime that lets you be taxed as a non-resident during your arrival year and the following five (up to 6 years), at a flat 24% rate on employment income up to €600,000 a year, instead of the progressive IRPF scale, which can exceed 47%.

02

Why it matters if you're coming from LATAM

You're only taxed in Spain on Spanish-source income; your income, dividends or wealth in your home country generally fall outside Spanish taxation. On top of that, there's no obligation to file the "Modelo 720" on assets held abroad.

03

Who it applies to

Employees, entrepreneurs, qualified professionals, company directors and international remote workers, and, under certain conditions, their spouse and children. Key requirement: not having been a Spanish tax resident in the previous 5 years.

04

Where we come in

There's a 6-month application window from your registration with the Seguridad Social, and missing it closes the door to the regime. At Guimaes we assess whether it suits you, handle the application (Modelos 149 and 151) and follow up throughout the whole period.

What the landing process looks like

Setting up in another country involves decisions that depend on one another: the legal form affects taxation, your personal taxes depend on when you move and the visa depends on how the investment is structured. That is why we don't treat it as a list of separate formalities, but as a project with phases.

  • Before you arrive. We analyse your project, your home country and the relationship between the parent company and the future Spanish company, and propose the structure and timetable.
  • Incorporation and registrations. We set up the company, handle the tax registrations and support you in opening the bank account.
  • First months. We set up accounting and periodic taxes and, if you are going to hire, labor management and payroll for your first team.
  • Ongoing support. We continue as your advisers in Spain, with the same point of contact, so that growth doesn't force you to change teams.

Decisions worth taking before incorporating

The question is not just how to open a company in Spain, but which company to open. Before signing anything we review with you whether a subsidiary, a branch or a new company held by the founders makes more sense; how profits will flow between Spain and your country, and which double taxation treaty applies; who will be the director and where they will reside; and, if there are several partners, how their relationship is governed from day one with a shareholder agreement.

Sorting this out at the start avoids costly restructuring later. If you are still unsure whether to operate as an individual or through a company, it will help to read sole trader or limited company?.

FAQ

Questions about landing in Spain

Do I need to be physically in Spain to set up the company?+

Not necessarily. Much of the process can be done with notarial powers of attorney and online procedures; we tell you exactly when your presence is advisable.

Do you help with visas and work permits?+

Yes. We guide you to the most suitable route (investor, entrepreneur or intra-company transfer visa) and coordinate the paperwork with immigration specialists.

How long does the landing process take?+

Incorporation is usually done within a few weeks; the full timetable (bank, tax, first hires) depends on your case and we set it out from the first meeting.

Subsidiary, branch or new company?+

It depends on your structure back home, how you want to repatriate profits and the applicable treaty. We analyse it with you before incorporating so you don't have to restructure later.

Do you keep supporting us after incorporation?+

Yes, that is the point of the 360º service: we remain your tax, accounting and labor advisers in Spain, with the same contact from day one.

Do you work with companies from any Latin American country?+

Yes, we support companies and founders from across Latin America, adapting the structure to your home country and the applicable double taxation treaties.
