What an outsourced CFO is

An outsourced CFO is a finance director who works for your company part-time, without being on the payroll. They take on the functions of financial management —controlling cash, interpreting the numbers, planning and supporting major decisions— with the commitment your company needs at each moment: a few hours a month in a stable phase, more involvement when a transaction is under way.

Depending on who describes it, you will see it called a fractional CFO, outsourced finance director, outsourced financial management or external financial adviser. It is the same idea: giving an SME access to the judgement of an experienced finance director without the cost or the risk of a full-time hire.

When an SME needs an outsourced finance director

There is no turnover figure above which it becomes compulsory. What sets the moment is complexity and what is at stake. It usually makes sense when the company is growing but you don't know in advance whether you will have cash next month, when big decisions are made by gut feeling, when a bank or investor asks for information nobody in the company knows how to prepare, or when the founder spends more time putting out financial fires than running the business.

We have gathered those signs, as well as the cases where it doesn't pay off yet, in the article when your SME needs an outsourced CFO (and when not).

A CFO for founders with no finance team

This is one of the most common cases. Startups and young companies where the founder keeps the numbers in a spreadsheet, the bookkeeping is done by an outside firm and nobody looks at the whole picture. An outsourced CFO brings order without building a department: designing the financial model, setting up simple monthly reporting, getting the company ready to talk to investors and banks, and giving you someone to test every decision against the numbers.

What an outsourced CFO does day to day

  • Controls cash. Builds and maintains a cash forecast that warns you weeks in advance of any strain, so you can act in time. We explain it in detail in cash flow and liquidity.
  • Turns accounting into decision-making information. Margins by product, customer or business line, and a dashboard with the indicators that drive your company.
  • Plans. Annual budget, forecasts and scenarios so that investment, hiring or pricing decisions are made with the numbers in front of you.
  • Manages funding. Relationship with banks, negotiating terms, debt structure and preparing the information they request.
  • Reports. Prepares information for the board, partners or investors, on a regular and consistent basis.
  • Supports transactions. Funding rounds, buying or selling companies, new partners or restructurings, together with our strategic planning team.

Outsourced CFO, bookkeeper or in-house CFO: how they differ

Compared with a bookkeeper or accountant. The bookkeeper records activity and meets tax and corporate obligations: essential work that looks at the past. The outsourced CFO uses those numbers to look ahead: anticipating cash, planning and deciding. They don't compete, they complement each other, and they work best when coordinated. At Guimaes, accounting and financial management sit in the same team.

Compared with an in-house CFO. A finance director on the payroll is fully dedicated and knows the business day to day, but also carries a high fixed cost and a long recruitment process. For most SMEs, the financial workload doesn't justify a full-time position. An outsourced CFO offers the same level of judgement with a tailored commitment that you can scale up or down as the company evolves. When the company grows to the point of needing an in-house CFO, the outsourced one can help define the role and manage the transition.

How to hire an outsourced CFO

Hiring an outsourced CFO is simpler than it seems, but it is worth asking a few questions before choosing:

  • What specific problem do you want to solve? Controlling cash, preparing a round or professionalising reporting require different levels of commitment.
  • Do they have experience with companies like yours? In size, sector and the stage you are at.
  • How do they coordinate with your accounting and tax? If the CFO works in isolation, someone will have to bridge the gap between them.
  • What will you receive and when? A clear schedule of deliverables —cash forecast, monthly report, follow-up meetings— avoids misunderstandings.
  • Is there a lock-in? A good outsourced CFO stays because they add value, not because of a contract.

What's the difference between an outsourced CFO and my accountant?+

Accounting looks at the past and meets obligations; the CFO looks ahead and helps you decide: cash forecasting, funding, margins and strategy. The two complement each other.

When does an SME need an outsourced finance director?+

When complexity outgrows in-house capacity: you are growing without cash visibility, you are about to seek funding or investment, or you make major decisions without numbers to back them.
